Olympics 2026 – Travelling by car to the Winter Games in Italy

At a glance

  • From 6 to 22 February 2026, the Olympic Winter Games will take place in Northern Italy.
  • The competitions will be spread across several venues between Milan and the Dolomites.
  • Alongside urban venues in Milan, alpine events in well-known winter sports regions will play a central role.
  • During the Games, special traffic and access regulations will apply around the Olympic venues.
  • In certain areas, access for motorists will only be permitted with a valid car pass.

Olympics 2026 in Milan & Cortina

From 6 to 22 February 2026, Northern Italy will turn into the heart of the winter sports world.

Milan and the Dolomites’ iconic resort town of Cortina d’Ampezzo will welcome holidaymakers, sports fans and road trip enthusiasts to one of the most extraordinary winter journeys of the year.

The opening ceremony will take place in Milan, while many of the most spectacular events – including alpine skiing – will be held in the Dolomites around Cortina d’Ampezzo.
Additional venues such as Bormio, Livigno and Antholz will be spread across Northern Italy and South Tyrol.

Why the 2026 Olympics are perfect for a road trip

Unlike previous Winter Games, Milano Cortina 2026 is not centred around a single location but spread across several regions. Travelling by car allows visitors to connect different venues and combine the Olympic experience with a more individual, flexible stay.

Driving to Italy: Tolls & traffic regulations

Is a vignette required in Italy?

❌ No. Italy does not use a traditional motorway vignette.

➜ Instead, tolls are distance-based and calculated according to the number of kilometres driven.

✅ Payment is made either:

  • at toll booths (ticket & barrier), or
  • on selected routes via electronic free-flow systems

You can find further information about tolls in Italy here.

Olympics & traffic: What you should know on site

During the Games, special traffic regulations will apply in many host locations:

  • ZTL zones (restricted traffic areas), for example in Milan

  • Temporary access restrictions around sports venues

  • In some cases, access only with a car pass or special permit

  • Limited parking availability, especially in mountain regions

💡 Tip: Choose accommodation and parking options outside the main event hotspots where possible, and allow plenty of extra time for travel.

Winter road trip through Northern Italy: Preparation is key

❄️ Winter tyres are mandatory in many regions

🏔️ Snow chains may be required locally

🚧 Weather-related closures of individual Alpine passes are possible

⏱️ Allow extra time on competition days
