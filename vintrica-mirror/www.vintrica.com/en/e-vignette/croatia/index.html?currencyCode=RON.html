Vignette Croatia

All vehicles using the motorway network in Croatia are required to pay a toll. The amount depends on the distance travelled, the vehicle category, and the specific motorway. Since Croatia joined the Eurozone on 1 January 2023, tolls can be paid in euros. Payment is possible directly at the toll station — either in cash, by debit card, or by credit card. Alternatively, an electronic toll device such as the vintrica Flow Box can be used, which enables automatic billing.

Since June 2020, all bridges in Croatia have been exempt from an extra toll. The Krk Bridge and Mirna Bridge are among the general toll roads. Since then, there has only been one toll tunnel, the Ucka Tunnel.

Does Croatia have a vignette?

At present, a distance-based toll system still applies in Croatia. A digital toll system with e-vignettes and toll boxes was originally scheduled for early 2025, but its launch has been postponed to 2026.

However, an electronic solution already exists in the form of the so-called ENC system (Elektronička Naplata Cestarine), which functions similarly to the ETC (Electronic Toll Collect) systems used in other countries. With this system, tolls are recorded automatically, eliminating the need to stop at toll stations. The new system, set to launch in 2026, aims to improve traffic flow on Croatian motorways and ease congestion at toll booths.

Tolls and payment

If you are travelling without an ENC-compatible device, you must stop at a toll station at every motorway entry point and take a ticket. You must keep this ticket until you exit the motorway and pay the toll there. The toll ticket records the distance travelled and your vehicle category.

Unfortunately, it is not possible to determine the exact toll amount in advance, and there is no standardised pricing across motorway sections. However, you can roughly expect costs of around £7 per 100 km for category 1 vehicles.

Eletronic Toll Collect - ETC

As an alternative to traditional ticket-based payment, you can use the so-called ENC system. With this method, tolls are processed cashlessly and without stopping, using a small transponder mounted on the windscreen. The transponder is rechargeable and can be used without any time restrictions.

In addition to the solutions offered by the Croatian motorway operator HAC, the vintrica Flow Box is also compatible with the ENC system and enables easy, automatic toll payment. Furthermore, the vintrica Flow Box can also be used in other Southern European countries such as Italy, France, Spain, and Portugal.

When is an ETC worthwhile?

Purchasing the device is especially worthwhile if you expect to pay more than £ 60  in tolls – for example, on a round trip or holiday journey using the motorway in both directions. With the ENC device, you can receive a discount of up to 21% on standard toll rates, depending on the route.

How are tolls controlled in Croatia?

The Croatian toll is always controlled at the exits.

Fees

As vehicles in Croatia cannot enter the motorway without taking a toll ticket, which must be paid before leaving the motorway, no fines can be levied here. This means that the system is significantly less control-intensive for the Croatian police than the systems in Austria or Slovenia, for example. Unfortunately, there is still no video toll system in Croatia that could be used effectively.

Who is exempt from a vignette?

In Croatia, persons with an established physical disability of at least 80% of the lower extremities (from the hip upwards), HRVI (Croatian Disabled Homeland War Veterans) with a proven physical disability of 100% and persons with a visual impairment of 100% or cars in their possession are exempt from the car toll. Further information on toll-exempt vehicles can be found here at HAC.
